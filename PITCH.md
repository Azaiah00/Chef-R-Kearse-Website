# What we built for you, Chef

One page. Everything below is live and you can click all of it today.

---

## The problem you described

People waste your time. They enquire, you answer, you send a menu, and they were never going to book. Meanwhile the enquiries that *would* have booked wait behind them.

So we built two things: a website that only sends you people worth talking to, and an office that runs the ones who are.

---

## 1. Your enquiries now sort themselves

Every enquiry that comes through the website is scored out of 100 the moment it arrives, on nine things that actually predict whether somebody books — their budget, how far out the date is, whether they are the one who decides, how much they bothered to write, whether they have accepted that a date is held with a deposit.

Then it goes into one of four lanes:

| | What happens | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| **Priority** | Straight to you, flagged on your dashboard, reply within four hours | Your full attention, on the right people |
| **Qualified** | Your assistant screens it first and hands it over with a summary | A few minutes of hers |
| **Nurture** | Automatic reply with your menus, added to your seasonal list | Nothing |
| **Not a fit** | Gracious automatic no, with your newsletter attached | **Nothing at all** |

Nobody is ever rude to anybody. Every single person gets a courteous answer, and nobody ever sees a score. You just stop spending evenings on the ones who were never going to book.

**In the demo, six of ten enquiries needed you. Four handled themselves.**

You can also disagree with it. If the system scores somebody low and your gut says otherwise, you override it — and it records why, so over time the scoring learns from the calls you actually made.

---

## 2. Your own office, and a different one for your assistant

Two of you sign in at the same place and get completely different screens, because you are doing different jobs.

**Yours** answers "what is the state of my business and what will break if I ignore it":

- **Needs you today** — unanswered guests, unpaid deposits, menus not locked close to service, run-sheet items slipping. Computed, not a list you have to maintain.
- Your next events, each with the four things that actually ruin one: deposit in, menu locked, headcount confirmed, allergies written down.
- What is on the books, what is out for decision, your average event.
- **Your next eight weeks** at a glance, with open Saturdays highlighted — and when one is inside three weeks, your marketing has already queued something for it.
- Where you are losing work and what it cost you.

**Hers** answers "what do I have to get done":

- Her day, sorted into *behind*, *today* and *this week*, with each item written as the thing to actually do — "chase the deposit", not "deposit: outstanding".
- Run-sheets per event showing only her jobs.
- Guests waiting on a reply, with one tap to call or email.

**She sees no money by default.** Not your quotes, not your bookings, not your deposits. You switch that on for her the day you want to. It is off until then.

---

## 3. Your clients get their own page

Every client gets a private link — no password, no app, nothing to download. On it:

- Their event, a countdown, and **exactly where things stand** — what is done, what is waiting on you, what is waiting on them
- A direct conversation with you and the office, and they can see which of you replied
- Their day-of timings and who is looking after them

Most of the "just checking in" emails you answer are somebody who cannot see the state of their own booking. Show them, and those emails stop.

---

## 4. They build their own menu

On that same page they choose their courses **from photographs of your actual food**. Not a list of names — your plates.

Then the part nobody else in this market does: they tell you who they are feeding — the vegetarian, the shellfish allergy, the guest who avoids pork — and the page **flags any dish that clashes with any guest, before the menu is finished.** Nothing is blocked; you plate around this all the time. But you know in March instead of on the night.

They submit it, you comment on the courses, you lock it. Once it is locked nobody can change it, which is what lets you order against it.

---

## 5. The locked menu becomes your shopping list

The moment you lock a menu, it turns into:

- **A shopping list**, grouped by where you stand in the shop, scaled to the exact headcount
- **A prep schedule** counted backwards from service — real times, including "Day before, 4:00 PM"

Both download to your phone.

One honest note: the ingredient quantities in there right now are sensible estimates, not your recipes, because we have not had your recipes. Give us your component lists and yields and this stops being a clever demo and starts saving you an hour before every event.

## 6. Your marketing is written and waiting

Nineteen finished pieces, in the portal, ready to download right now:

- **Ten emails** — corporate holiday dinners, weddings, venue coordinators, tasting invitations, open dates, win-backs, your seasonal menu drop, and the review and referral asks. Designed, brand-styled, written in your voice.
- **Two paid advertising plans** — the actual headlines, the actual copy, who to target, what to spend, and how to tell whether it worked.
- **Six campaigns** covering the corporate and wedding work you said you want more of.

And the tab updates itself every Monday. It looks at your open dates and the season, queues what should go out this week, and tells you *why* it picked each one. **Nothing sends until you have read it and approved it.** That is not negotiable in this build — your name is on it.
