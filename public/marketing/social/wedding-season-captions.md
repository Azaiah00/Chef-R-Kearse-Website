# Instagram — Wedding Season

Eight captions and four story frames. Written to be posted as they stand.

**Voice:** plain, warm, unshowy. No emoji, no exclamation marks, no hashtag walls. Weddings are the one category where over-selling reads worst — a couple can tell when they are being worked on.

**Posting rhythm:** one a week, year round, two a week from January to April when couples who got engaged over the holidays start booking suppliers.

---

## 1 — The reframe

> Ask anyone about a wedding they went to. They will tell you about the food.
>
> Not the chairs. Not the flowers. Not the stationery. They will tell you whether the food was good, and they will remember it for years.
>
> Worth knowing when you are deciding what to protect in the budget.

**Pair with:** `lamb-chops-asparagus`.

---

## 2 — Why wedding food has the reputation it has

> Most wedding catering is built to be safe at scale. Cooked early, held warm, plated fast, engineered so nothing can go badly wrong.
>
> It is a reasonable way to feed a hundred people. It is also exactly why wedding food has the reputation it has.
>
> I cook on site and plate to order. It is more work and it is the entire difference.

**Pair with:** `snapper-mango-salsa`.

---

## 3 — Real proof, attributed

> "The food was fresh, tasty and beautifully presented. All of our guests raved about the delicious meal, and many said it was the best food they had ever had at a wedding."
>
> Marie R. Zola, March 2026.
>
> That last line is the one I care about.

**Pair with:** `lobster-shrimp-scampi`.

**Rule:** real reviews only, with source and date on the post. Never tidy one up into something better than it was.

---

## 4 — The question nobody asks early enough

> Ask your venue whether you can bring in your own chef. Ask it before you fall in love with the room.
>
> Some venues allow it, some do not, and some allow it with conditions about load-in and power that are worth knowing while you still have options.
>
> If yours allows it, I will come and walk the kitchen with you and tell you honestly what is possible in that space. Sometimes the answer is that the menu has to change. Better to hear that in March than at six o'clock on the day.

**Pair with:** `venue-kitchen-gen` or `wedding-barn-golden-gen`.

---

## 5 — Two mains

> Two mains is not an extravagance.
>
> At most headcounts it costs very little more, and it changes how people feel about the meal, because nobody is eating something they would not have chosen.
>
> I take the split with the invitations so nothing is wasted.

**Pair with:** `lamb-chops-asparagus` or `lobster-tail-plated`.

---

## 6 — Eat at your own wedding

> You will not get to taste any of it.
>
> Everybody tells you that and everybody is right. You will be talking to people from the moment you sit down.
>
> So I put a plate aside for the two of you somewhere quiet, and somebody comes and finds you when it is there. Ten minutes, just the two of you, eating the food you spent months choosing.
>
> It is the part couples thank me for afterwards.

**Pair with:** `wedding-place-setting-gen` or `crab-stuffed-plated`.

---

## 7 — For coordinators and planners

> For the venue coordinators and planners who see this:
>
> I walk your kitchen before the day and I tell you honestly if what the couple wants is not possible in your space. I bring my own servers and bartenders. I set up and I break down completely. I do not go around you to the couple, and I do not undercut your preferred list.
>
> If it is useful I will come out, look at the space, and leave you a one-page note on what is realistic to cook there. No charge and no obligation.

**Pair with:** `venue-kitchen-gen` or `table-setting`.

---

## 8 — The allergy one

> Tell me about allergies at the start.
>
> At one wedding this year a guest carried an EpiPen for shellfish and there was crab on the second course. So her first course was plated separately, different board, different gloves, and it went out on a marked plate so the server knew before they picked it up.
>
> Her niece told me afterwards that the last caterer had made them feel like a nuisance for asking.
>
> Nobody should feel like a nuisance for wanting to eat safely at a wedding.

**Pair with:** `garden-salad` or `wedding-place-setting-gen`.

**Note before posting:** this references a real booking. Get the couple's permission, or change it to the general version with no detail that identifies anybody. Never invent an anecdote like this.

## What not to post

- No generated image of a wedding. No couples, no guests, no first dances. A generated wedding photograph implies he catered a wedding that never happened, and a couple who works that out has every right to be furious.
- No generated image of a dish, in any campaign, ever.
- No testimonial without a name, a source and a date.
- No comparison against a named venue or a named caterer.
- No emoji.

---

## The post that would be worth more than all eight of these

A real wedding he catered, photographed properly. Ask the next couple for permission in writing in advance and bring a photographer for two hours — the room before, the plates going out, the carving, the table full.

One real set beats every generated plate and every caption above, and it can be used for years.
