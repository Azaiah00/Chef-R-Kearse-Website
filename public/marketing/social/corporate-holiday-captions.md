# Instagram — Corporate Holiday Parties

Nine captions and four story frames. Written to be posted as they stand.

**Voice note:** Chef Kearse's own writing is plain and unshowy — "Your invitation to the perfect catered affair" is his line, and it is warm without being loud. These are written to match: short sentences, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no hashtag walls. Confidence reads as calm, not volume.

**Posting rhythm:** two a week through October and November. Alternate between a dish photograph and a message post, so the feed does not read as one long advert.

**Hashtags:** eight is plenty and they go on the first comment, not in the caption. A wall of thirty tags makes a business look like it is shouting. Suggested set at the bottom.

---

## 1 — The opener

> Most company dinners are a room you rent and a menu you pick from three options.
>
> Nobody complains about them. Nobody remembers them either.
>
> I bring the kitchen to your own space instead. Cooked on site, plated to order, and I come out to talk to your table about what they are eating. My team serves, tends the bar, and leaves the place so clean that whoever locks up has nothing to do.
>
> December fills from the outside in. If you know your date, tell me now.

**Pair with:** `smoked-beef-sliced` or the generated `corporate-office-table-gen` plate.

## 2 — The thing people photograph

> Eight hours of smoke, then carved at your table.
>
> The carving is the part people take pictures of. I have stopped sending it out already plated because the room is better when it happens in front of everybody.

**Pair with:** `smoked-beef-sliced`.

## 3 — What the enquiry actually takes

> Two things and I can tell you whether I am free.
>
> A date and a headcount.
>
> That is it. If your date is open I come back with a menu built for your room and your people. If it is not, I tell you straight away instead of leaving you waiting a week.

**Pair with:** `table-setting` or `surface-dark-warm-gen` with the text set over it.

## 4 — For the person who got handed this job

> If somebody has handed you "sort out the holiday party" on top of your actual job, this is for you.
>
> You do not need to know what you want. You need one supplier who will not embarrass you.
>
> Send me a date, a number of people, and roughly what you can spend a head. I will come back with one menu and one price with nothing hidden underneath it, including everybody with a dietary requirement, so you can forward it to whoever signs things.

**Pair with:** `pasta-wine-candle` or `kitchen-pass-gen`.

## 5 — The size question

> Thirty to sixty people: plated dinner in your own space. Three courses, two choices on the main, nobody eating something they would not have picked.
>
> Sixty to a hundred and fifty: stations. Something raw, something from the water, something carved in front of people. Keeps the room moving instead of seated in rows.
>
> Under thirty: this is where it gets interesting. At that size I can cook things I would not attempt for a hundred.

**Pair with:** `seafood-crab-roast` or `stuffed-shrimp-platter`.

---

## 6 — Proof, with the source on it

> "Every little detail was well thought out and the service was exceptional. Everything was amazing."
>
> Beth, private event. Fash, March 2021.
>
> The detail is most of the job. Anyone can cook one good plate. Forty of them, at the same time, at the right temperature, in somebody else's kitchen, is a different thing.

**Pair with:** `crab-stuffed-plated`.

**Rule:** real reviews only, with the source and the date on the post. Never paraphrase one into something better than it was.

---

## 7 — The allergy one

> Tell me about allergies at the start, not two weeks out.
>
> A guest with a serious allergy should be planned for properly — separate board, separate gloves, a marked plate so the server knows before they pick it up. That takes a conversation, not a note on a spreadsheet the night before.
>
> It is also the thing that tells your people you actually thought about them.

**Pair with:** `garden-salad` or `kitchen-pass-gen`.

---

## 8 — The honest urgency

> What is actually still open in December, since vague urgency is worse than none:
>
> Fridays in the second and third weeks go first every year, and they are going. Weeknights in the first two weeks are wide open — and they are genuinely the better evening, because people are not exhausted yet and nobody is racing off to another party.

**Pair with:** `surface-dark-warm-gen` with the text set over it, or `lobster-tail-plated`.

---

## 9 — The close

> Last one about December.
>
> If you have a date, send it. If you do not, that is fine — I will be here in January, and the first quarter is a much better time to feed a team properly anyway. Nobody is competing for the diary and everybody has more attention to give it.

**Pair with:** `room-after-gen`.

## What not to post

- No countdown graphics or "only 3 spots left" unless it is literally true. In a market this small, one person who books and finds out the scarcity was invented tells other people.
- No generated image of a dish. Ever. If there is no photograph of it, it does not get posted.
- No before-and-after comparisons against a named competitor or venue.
- No emoji. It is not the voice, and it does not read as premium.
