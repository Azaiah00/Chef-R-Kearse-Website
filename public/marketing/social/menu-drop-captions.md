# Seasonal Menu Drop — captions and carousel plan

Four times a year, on the turn of each season. This is the campaign that keeps the list warm between events, and it is the one that gets forwarded.

**The principle:** menu first, sell second. There is nothing to buy in a menu drop. That is exactly why people read it, and it is why the open rate on the email version is the highest of any campaign in the programme.

---

## Caption for the carousel

> Four times a year I write a new menu and send it to my list before it goes anywhere else.
>
> There is nothing to buy in this post. Swipe through, and if you see something you want, remember it for whenever you next have a reason to put people around a table.
>
> To start: chowder, properly thick, with the clam liquor in it rather than just cream. Or mango and sweet pepper salsa, cut fine and dressed an hour before it is eaten.
>
> From the water: roasted fish under mango and pepper. Lobster tail split and broiled in butter, plain, because it does not need anything else. Shrimp and smoked sausage over stone-ground grits with sharp cheese.
>
> From the fire: beef, dry-brined overnight, smoked eight hours, carved at the table. Herb-crusted lamb with charred asparagus and a roasted lemon.
>
> To finish: creme brulee, torched at the table. Strawberries with cream and shortcake, which is not a clever dessert and is not trying to be.
>
> Every menu gets written for the event it is cooked for, so none of this is fixed. If you want something on it and something off it, say so. That is how these evenings should work.

## Five single posts, spaced through the quarter

One a week after the drop, so the menu keeps working rather than being one post that scrolls away.

### 1 — The dish that gets asked for by name

> This one has become the dish people ask for by name.
>
> A whole fillet, roasted, then buried in mango, sweet pepper, red onion and cilantro. The salsa gets cut fine and dressed an hour before it goes out, so it has softened slightly but still has bite.
>
> Fewer things on the plate, better sourcing, more heat. That is most of what I have changed this year.

**Pair with:** `snapper-mango-salsa`.

### 2 — The technical one

> Eight hours of smoke, and the part that actually matters happens the day before.
>
> Trimmed, rubbed, then left uncovered in the fridge overnight. That dries the surface, which is the only reason the bark forms properly. Skip it and you get a good piece of beef with a soft outside.
>
> Then the fire, then a long rest, then carved in front of everybody.

**Pair with:** `smoked-beef-sliced`.

### 3 — The simple one

> Split, butter, broil, lemon.
>
> That is the entire recipe, and anything else on the plate is getting in the way.
>
> Most of learning to cook is adding things. The rest of it is taking them off again.

**Pair with:** `lobster-tail-plated`.

### 4 — Weekly service, which is the quiet success of the year

> Something I have started doing for a few households: four or five dinners cooked and stored, one day a week.
>
> It is not glamorous and nobody photographs it. It is also the thing that has changed the most lives out of anything I do — people who were eating badly because their weeks are too full, now eating properly without thinking about it.
>
> Consistency matters more than variety for this. Same few things, done well, on a Sunday.

**Pair with:** `smothered-chicken` or `garden-salad`.

### 5 — The close of the quarter

> Last one from this menu before the next season.
>
> If any of it stuck with you, the whole thing is at the link, and the next drop goes out at the turn of the season.

**Pair with:** `dessert-strawberries`.

## Why this campaign matters more than it looks

It has the lowest direct conversion of anything in the programme, and it is the one to protect.

The seasonal drop is what keeps him in mind during the ten months of the year when someone is not planning an event. When they finally do need a chef, he is the one they already know — which means the booking costs nothing to win.

It is also the email people forward, which makes it the cheapest list growth available. A forwarded menu from someone you trust converts better than any advertisement he could buy.

Judge it on list growth and on how many bookings arrive from people who have been on the list for more than six months. Not on clicks in the week it goes out.
