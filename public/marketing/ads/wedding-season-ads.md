# Paid social — Wedding Season

Three concepts for couples in Central Virginia and the DMV, plus the one organic play that is worth more than all three.

**Season:** heaviest January to April, when couples who got engaged over the holidays start booking suppliers. A second, smaller push in September and October for the following spring.

**Who you are actually talking to:** a couple who have booked a venue, or are about to, and are now working through a supplier list. They have a budget that is already stretched and they are deciding what to protect. Your entire job in these ads is to make the food the thing they protect.

---

## The strategic point, stated first

Paid social for weddings is expensive and competitive, and a chef cannot outspend a venue group. So the honest advice is this: **run a modest paid budget, and put the real effort into venue coordinators.**

One coordinator who trusts him puts him in front of every couple who books that venue, for years, at no media cost. The `wedding-03-venue-partner.html` email and the walkthrough offer inside it will almost certainly out-earn every dollar below. Run the ads, but do not mistake them for the strategy.

**Suggested opening spend:** $15–25 a day total across Concepts A and B for three weeks, then move everything to Concept C (retargeting) once it has an audience of 300 or more.

**Geography:** Richmond metro, Charlottesville, Northern Virginia, DC, and the Maryland suburbs. Wedding venues pull from further than restaurants do, so a wider radius is justified here than for corporate.

**Targeting:** engaged, 12-month and 6-month engagement windows where the platform offers them. Layer interests in food and restaurants rather than in weddings — a couple who follow chefs are the couple who will pay for a chef.

**Exclusions:** anyone who has enquired. Anyone who has attended his page's events.

---

## Concept A — What people actually remember

**The insight it runs on:** every couple is being sold flowers, chairs, stationery and a photographer. Nobody is telling them which of those their guests will still be talking about in five years. Say it plainly and it reframes the whole budget.

**Format:** single image, 4:5 feed and 9:16 stories.

**Image:** `/images/dishes/lamb-chops-asparagus-1600.webp` — his own photograph. Warm, close, real.

**Headline**
> Ask anyone about a wedding they went to. They will tell you about the food.

**Body**
> Not the chairs. Not the flowers. A private chef who cooks on site and plates to order, for weddings across Virginia, DC and Maryland.
>
> Built around what you two actually like to eat.

**Call to action:** See the wedding menus

**Destination:** `/weddings`

**Why it should work:** it does not claim to be better than anyone. It moves the budget conversation, which is a much easier argument to win than a quality one.

## Concept B — The real review

**The insight it runs on:** a couple trusts another couple. Nothing he writes about himself competes with what a bride already wrote about him.

**Format:** single image with the quote set as type over a darkened photograph, 4:5 and 9:16. Alternatively a carousel: quote on frame one, dish on frame two, the ask on frame three.

**Image:** `/images/dishes/lobster-shrimp-scampi-1600.webp`, darkened to about 45 percent, quote set in the display serif over it. Or use the generated atmosphere plate `wedding-02` as the background if a plainer surface reads better with the type.

**The quote — use it exactly, with attribution**
> "Many said it was the best food they had ever had at a wedding."
>
> — Marie R., Zola, March 2026

**Body**
> A private chef for your wedding, not a banquet kitchen. Cooked on site, plated to order, served and cleared by his own team.
>
> Central Virginia and the DMV.

**Call to action:** See if your date is free

**Destination:** `/book?service=wedding`

**Compliance note that matters:** this is a real, attributed, dated review from a real platform. Do not shorten it in a way that changes its meaning, do not remove the attribution, and never run a testimonial he has not actually received. Fabricated social proof is both the fastest way to lose a licence to advertise and the fastest way to lose a reputation in a market this small.

## Concept C — Retargeting: the question they are stuck on

**Audience:** visited `/weddings`, `/menus` or `/gallery` in the last 45 days, did not enquire. Wedding consideration windows are long, so use 45 days rather than 30.

**Format:** single image, 4:5.

**Image:** `/images/dishes/crab-stuffed-golden-1600.webp` or the generated plate `wedding-04` (an empty dressed table at golden hour).

**Headline**
> Can you even bring your own chef?

**Body**
> Ask your venue before you decide anything else — some allow it and some do not, and it is worth knowing early. If yours does, he will walk the kitchen with you and tell you honestly what is possible in that space.
>
> Then a date and a headcount is all the enquiry takes.

**Call to action:** Check your date

**Destination:** `/book?service=wedding`

**Why it should work:** it answers the actual unspoken blocker. A couple who like the food but assume they are locked into the venue's caterer never enquire, and no amount of beautiful photography moves them. This does.

## The organic play that beats all of it

**Venue coordinator outreach.** Build a list of every wedding venue within ninety minutes — barns, estates, hotels, museums, wineries, city venues. For each one, find the coordinator by name.

Send `wedding-03-venue-partner.html`, individually, with the venue's name in the first line. Not a mailshot.

The offer inside it is the whole point: he will come to the venue, walk the kitchen, and leave a one-page note on what is realistic to cook there. Free, no obligation. Most chefs will not do this, it costs him an afternoon, and it makes him the person the coordinator recommends when a couple asks — because he is the only supplier who has bothered to learn their kitchen.

Target twenty venues. Expect two or three relationships. Two coordinator relationships is a wedding season.

---

## Measuring it honestly

Weddings have a long lag. An enquiry in February books an event in October and the deposit lands somewhere in between, so judging a wedding campaign on this month's bookings is meaningless.

Track instead:

1. **Cost per A or B band wedding enquiry.** Knowable within a day of the click.
2. **Tasting conversion.** Enquiry to tasting is the real qualifying step for weddings — it is where a couple commits their evening to him.
3. **Where the booked weddings came from.** After a season, if referrals and coordinators produced the bookings and paid social produced enquiries that never converted, move the whole budget. That is not a failure of the ads; it is what this market usually looks like, and finding it out is worth the spend.
