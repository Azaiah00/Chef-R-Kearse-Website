Weddings

Ask anyone about a wedding they went to. They will tell you about the food.

They will not remember the chair covers or the colour of the napkins. They will remember whether the food was good, and they will remember it for years.

Most wedding catering is built to be safe at scale. Cooked early, held warm, plated fast, engineered so that nothing can go badly wrong. It is a reasonable way to feed a hundred people and it is why wedding food has the reputation it has.

I work the other way round. I cook on site and I plate to order, and I build the menu around what you two actually like to eat rather than around a package.

“The food was fresh, tasty and beautifully presented. All of our guests raved about the delicious meal, and many said it was the best food they had ever had at a wedding.”
Marie R. · wedding · Zola, March 2026
Built around what the couple eats, not around a package.

A few things worth knowing before you talk to anyone:

A venue's in-house caterer is the easy answer and sometimes the right one. Ask whether you are allowed to bring in a chef at all — some venues say no, and that is worth finding out before you fall in love with the room.

Two mains is not an extravagance. At most headcounts it costs very little more and it changes how people feel about the meal, because nobody is eating something they would not have chosen.

Tell your caterer about allergies at the start, not two weeks out. A guest with a serious allergy should be planned for, not worked around in a rush.

And eat at your own wedding. I will put a plate aside for the two of you somewhere quiet, because otherwise you genuinely will not get to taste any of it.
